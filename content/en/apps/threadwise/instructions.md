[Open the app](https://tf5nn.github.io/threadwise/). It opens on **Find**. The tabs run in workshop order, but each one works on its own: start wherever you like.

## Find: what is it?

1. Choose **Bolt** or **Nut or hole**.
2. Measure the diameter with a caliper: over the thread tops on a bolt, and inside on the thread tops in a nut (that is the minor diameter, not the size: an M10 nut measures about 8.4–8.7 mm).
3. Add the pitch: from a gauge in mm or TPI, or measure the length over 10 or more threads.
4. If you know the flank angle or whether it is tapered, add that. **Flank angle from two wire sizes** works it out from two over-wire readings.
5. The list is ranked closest first, with warnings for look-alikes and twins. Check the top few with a gauge or a known nut or bolt.
6. Tap a result (or its **Make it** button) to open it in Make.

## Make: the figures to cut it

1. Choose the thread, **External** or **Internal**, and the class (or arrive from Find with it filled in).
2. **Sizes** gives the diameter to turn or bore, and the pitch-diameter limits. Tapered threads show their gauge-plane sizes.
3. For internal threads, **Tap drill** lists drills near 75 % of thread; green ones fall inside the class's minor-diameter limits, and ★ marks the drill the *Töflubók* gives.
4. **Single-point threading** gives the depth, the compound angle (half the thread angle less ½°) and a pass schedule. Switch between straight radial infeed and the compound. (S buttress threads are straight-in only for now.)
5. **Measure it** takes a bolt on to Measure with the same size and class.

## Measure: is it right?

1. Choose the thread type and size, or type your own diameter and pitch (metric, unified and Whitworth).
2. Under **Wires**, measure the wires you have and type their diameter. The reading changes about three times as much as the wire does, so the wrong wire gives the wrong answer. The bar shows where your wire sits between the smallest and largest that work for that thread form.
3. Choose the tolerance class, or **Manual** for limits from a drawing.
4. Aim for the **target** (the middle of the tolerance). To check a part, type the micrometer reading under **Check a measurement**.
