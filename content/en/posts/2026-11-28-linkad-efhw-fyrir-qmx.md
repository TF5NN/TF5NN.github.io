Got a bit of time to finish the antenna for the QMX. It's a linked EFHW that I can put up in three ways:

1. Just one wire for 20 m, quick and easy.
2. The 20 m wire plus the extension, put up as an inverted V, which gives me 40 and 20 m.
3. The wire with the extension but the link open, which gives me 30 m.

![Sketch of the setup: the rig, 4 m of coax, the 49:1 UnUn, about 10 m of wire up to the mast for 20 m and the extension down the other side for 40 m, with a link for 30 m](/media/2026-01-qmx-efhw-skissa.png)

I could possibly also get 15 m with the whole wire up, and 10 m whichever configuration I have up 😆 But that doesn't matter, because the QMX only does 20, 30, 40, 60 and 80 m.

![The antenna up as an inverted V on a mast in the garden](/media/2026-01-qmx-efhw-1.jpg)

I found it interesting that I got the SWR on 40 and 20 m almost all the way down to 1, but the SWR on 30 m is 2. I suspect that's because I'm using 4 m of coax that also acts as the counterpoise. I chose that length because 5% of the wavelength of the lowest band you use is supposed to be enough for a counterpoise. That should then work harmonically with 40 and 20 m, but maybe doesn't suit 30 m as well.

![NanoVNA showing an SWR of 1.09 at 14.059 MHz](/media/2026-01-qmx-efhw-2.jpg)

![The link closed: the connectors pushed together on the wire](/media/2026-01-qmx-efhw-3.jpg)

![The link open for 30 m](/media/2026-01-qmx-efhw-4.jpg)

![The top of the mast: both wires attached to a carabiner](/media/2026-01-qmx-efhw-5.jpg)
